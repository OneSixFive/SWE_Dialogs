# Swedish B1 to B2 Progression for a Dialogue-Based Adult Learning App

## Introduction

Moving from B1 to B2 in Swedish means moving from “I can manage familiar situations and produce simple connected language” to “I can handle more complex input, interact with noticeably greater fluency, and explain a viewpoint with support, contrast, and detail.” In CEFR terms, B1 is still mostly about clear standard input on familiar matters and short connected production; B2 adds complex texts, sustained interaction without great strain, clearer argumentation, and more detailed writing. In Swedish, that shift usually shows up as better control of sentence architecture, more confident use of subordinate clauses and linking, wider everyday collocations, stronger listening tolerance for natural speed, and clearer pronunciation/prosody.

For an adult learner, this should not become a literature-heavy or exam-only track. The formal syllabi in Sweden include literature, source work, and language reflection, but the app-oriented version below converts those demands into practical adult communication: housing, appointments, public information, consumer issues, relationships, work/study routines, media, and civic life in Sweden. The aim is independent, real-world B2 communication, not specialized business jargon and not upper-secondary academic Swedish.

This progression synthesizes descriptors and syllabi from the Council of Europe, Skolverket, and Dalarna University. Key source documents were the official CEFR descriptors, the official ämnesplan for svenska som andraspråk, the B1.2 course outcomes, and the B2 syllabus and outcomes.

## Key learning principles

The progression should be built around purposeful communication, not around grammar lists alone. The weekly unit should always revolve around something an adult actually needs to do in Swedish: arrange, explain, compare, ask, solve, summarize, react, or argue. Grammar should appear first inside a dialogue or listening text, then be noticed, discussed briefly, and recycled in translation and production. Vocabulary should be taught as chunks and collocations rather than isolated nouns. Listening should move gradually from supportive standard speech to more natural pace, denser reasoning, and less scaffolding. Writing and speaking should stay short but frequent, because the B1→B2 jump depends heavily on automatizing patterns, not merely “knowing about” them. Stage 4 should therefore be a reinforcement stage: mixed retrieval, faster access, better repair strategies, and more confidence, rather than a new wave of heavy grammar.

A practical rule for this app is: one communicative goal, one main grammar focus, one lexical field, one listening challenge, and one production outcome per day. That keeps lessons JSON-friendly and prevents the B1 learner from being overloaded while still allowing repeated contact with B2-style functions such as argumentation, summarizing, and nuanced discussion.

## Progressive grammar syllabus

The grammar route from B1 to B2 should be progressive rather than exhaustive.

**Sentence architecture comes first.** The learner needs reliable control of Swedish main-clause inversion and fronting, then growing confidence with clause chaining and more subordinate structures. A useful early contrast is: *I dag jobbar jag hemma* — “Today I’m working from home” versus *Jag vet att jag inte jobbar hemma i morgon* — “I know that I’m not working from home tomorrow.” By B2, the learner should not just know these patterns but retrieve them quickly while speaking.

**Tense, time reference, and viewpoint form the next layer.** Swedish learners at this stretch need to stabilize the contrast between preteritum and perfekt, then add pluskvamperfekt for sequencing the past and more flexible future marking with *ska*, *kommer att*, and intention phrases. A core teaching contrast is: *Jag tappade nyckeln i går* — “I lost the key yesterday” versus *Jag har tappat nyckeln* — “I’ve lost the key.” The first reports a past event; the second foregrounds present relevance.

**Modality and stance should become more nuanced.** Early B1+ work should strengthen *kan, måste, får, borde, behöver*. Later stages should add softer stance language and hedging: *Det verkar som…*, *Jag antar att…*, *Det beror på…*, *Å ena sidan… å andra sidan…*. This is important because B2 requires viewpoint expression, explanation of options, and more precise interactional tone.

**Relative clauses, passives, and information packaging belong in the middle of the progression.** Start with everyday *som*-clauses such as *Mannen som jag pratade med…* and then broaden receptive awareness of more formal written variants like *vilken/vilket/vilka* and *vars*. Introduce passive voice in practical contexts first: notices, instructions, processes, public information, and news-style summaries. For example: *Biljetterna säljs online* — “The tickets are sold online,” and *Dörren blev stängd* — “The door was closed / got closed.”

**Cohesion and text logic are central at B2.** Learners must move beyond *och, men, därför att* toward a wider connector set for cause, consequence, contrast, concession, and conclusion: *eftersom, därför, samtidigt, trots att, däremot, dessutom, alltså, sammanfattningsvis*. They should also learn source-framing language such as *enligt artikeln*, *hon menar att*, and *det viktigaste är att…*, because B2 increasingly involves summarizing and reacting to spoken or written input.

**Pronunciation and prosody should run in parallel through all four stages.** The major goals are not “native-like accent” but intelligibility, stress/quantity control, clearer long–short vowel and consonant contrasts, safer sentence stress, and enough rhythm to sound organized and easy to follow. This matters especially in Swedish, where word stress, quantity, and assimilation patterns affect both comprehension and production.

In summary, the progressive grammar syllabus should move through:  
reliable core word order → narrative tense control → subordinate clause expansion → modal nuance → relative clauses and passives → cohesive argumentation and source-based language → mixed automatic control. Stage 4 should recycle those systems under time pressure and in integrated scenarios, not introduce a large new grammar load.

## Thematic vocabulary and communicative functions

B2 vocabulary growth in Swedish should be broad rather than specialized. The learner needs a bigger range for general adult life, more ability to vary formulations to avoid repetition, and better control of collocations. That means each theme should include verbs, nouns, adjectives, discourse markers, and reusable chunks together.

The strongest recurring thematic clusters for this progression are:

**Home, housing, and daily logistics:** *flytta, hyra, säga upp, boka tvättid, grannen, störande, boende, i närheten, pendla, komma för sent.*  
**Health and appointments:** *må bättre, ha ont i, boka om, undersökning, recept, vila, återhämta sig, symtom, vårdcentral.*  
**Consumer and service problems:** *kvitto, reklamation, öppettider, leverans, försenad, återbetalning, support, abonnemang, lösenord, uppkoppling.*  
**Work and study routines without jargon:** *deadline, kollega, schema, möte, uppgift, lämna in, förbereda sig, ansvar, samarbeta, feedback.*  
**Travel and transport:** *försening, byte, ställa in, perrong, avgång, boka biljett, resplan, trafik, kö, vägbeskrivning.*  
**Relationships and social life:** *komma överens, höra av sig, bjuda in, ställa upp, känna sig utanför, fira, umgås, sakna, oroa sig.*  
**News, culture, and opinions:** *artikel, inslag, handling, budskap, påverka, jämföra, fördel, nackdel, samhälle, vana.*  
**Digital and civic life:** *e-legitimation, blankett, myndighet, ansökan, regler, information, källa, trovärdig, dela, kommentera.*  

These choices fit the Swedish syllabus emphasis on communication in society, work, studies, source use, and reflection on language use, while also matching CEFR B2 expectations for wider general vocabulary, supportable viewpoints, and text-linked production.

The communicative functions should spiral upward across the 16 weeks:

describe routines and changes; narrate past events; explain reasons and results; compare options; give and ask for instructions; make requests more or less politely; express certainty and uncertainty; agree, partly agree, and disagree; summarize what someone else said; summarize a short source; report a problem and negotiate a solution; recommend and review; explain advantages and disadvantages; and sustain a viewpoint with examples. Useful B2-ready formulae include *Jag håller delvis med, men…* (“I partly agree, but…”), *Det beror på…* (“It depends on…”), *Skulle det vara möjligt att…* (“Would it be possible to…”), and *Sammanfattningsvis tycker jag att…* (“In summary, I think that…”).

## Four-stage progression plan

### Stage 1: Early B1+ Expansion

**Stage title:** **Stage 1: early B1+ expansion**

**CEFR-oriented goal:** consolidate the learner’s existing B1 base and push it into clear B1+, with the first visible B2 traits in listening, sentence control, and explanation.

**Main grammar/language targets:** main-clause inversion, negation placement, question patterns, preteritum versus perfekt, future expressions, modal verbs, comparisons, everyday relative clauses with *som*, and a first expansion of cohesive linkers.

**Vocabulary and thematic areas:** routines, plans, transport, shopping, home, appointments, family/social coordination, everyday problems and solutions.

**Communicative functions:** update someone, explain a change, ask follow-up questions, compare alternatives, make requests, give simple advice, narrate short recent events, express basic preferences with reasons.

**Listening goals:** understand clear standard Swedish at moderately natural speed on familiar adult topics; hear times, places, reasons, and practical details without needing constant repetition.

**Writing/production goals:** produce short connected messages, short descriptions, mini-narratives, and simple opinion paragraphs in understandable Swedish.

**Common learner difficulties:** English-influenced word order, weak distinction between *preteritum* and *perfekt*, overuse of basic connectors, uncertainty with collocations, and loss of accuracy when trying to speak faster.

**Four-week breakdown:** stabilize sentence architecture; stabilize time reference; build practical service language; then shift into comparison and short reasoned opinion.

### Stage 2: Solid B1+ to Low B2 Development

**Stage title:** **Stage 2: solid B1+ to low B2 development**

**CEFR-oriented goal:** move from connected everyday language to low-B2 performance in which the learner can explain, summarize, and argue more clearly across less predictable situations.

**Main grammar/language targets:** broader subordinate-clause use, reporting verbs and reported content, passive voice in practical contexts, stronger connector range, more deliberate formality control, and better paragraph logic in short texts.

**Vocabulary and thematic areas:** healthcare and administration, subscriptions and services, bills and support, workplace/study routines, local society, media, and consumer rights.

**Communicative functions:** explain causes and consequences, summarize an announcement or short source, report what someone said, complain politely, negotiate a solution, defend a preference, organize information for another person.

**Listening goals:** follow somewhat longer stretches of speech, short interviews, simple radio-style reports, and service dialogues where reasons, examples, and consequences matter.

**Writing/production goals:** write short complaint messages, short summaries, clearer emails, and two-paragraph pros/cons or explanation texts.

**Common learner difficulties:** maintaining correct clause order while speaking, using passive forms naturally, translating too literally from English, and sounding repetitive rather than varied.

**Four-week breakdown:** clause expansion; reporting and process language; consumer/civic communication; then low-B2 viewpoint building.

### Stage 3: B2-Level Complexity and Independence

**Stage title:** **Stage 3: B2-level complexity and independence**

**CEFR-oriented goal:** reach functional B2 performance in everyday adult Swedish: understand and discuss more complex input, sustain viewpoints, summarize sources, and interact with noticeably more independence.

**Main grammar/language targets:** flexible clause combination, better connector control, source-framing language, hedging and stance, conditionals/hypothetical reasoning, participle-based description, more varied formal/semi-formal phrasing.

**Vocabulary and thematic areas:** news and current affairs at everyday depth, culture and reviews, civic information, lifestyle choices, longer service problems, education/work discussions without jargon, and broad social topics.

**Communicative functions:** summarize and react to a source, defend a viewpoint, compare advantages and disadvantages, discuss a film/article/book, mediate information for someone else, respond to counterarguments, and make an explanation coherent over multiple turns.

**Listening goals:** follow longer explanations, podcasts or news-style clips on reasonably familiar topics, and discussions where the speaker’s position is not fully obvious from the first sentence.

**Writing/production goals:** write multi-paragraph opinion texts, reviews, summaries, and more deliberate formal or semi-formal messages.

**Common learner difficulties:** organization across longer speaking turns, underuse of cohesive devices, weak stance language, pronunciation dropping under speed, and lexical gaps that force repetition.

**Four-week breakdown:** source work; argument and counterargument; review/reaction and implied meaning; then integrated B2 adult scenarios.

### Stage 4: Consolidation, Fluency, Confidence, and Integrated Review

**Stage title:** **Stage 4: consolidation, fluency, confidence, and integrated review**

**CEFR-oriented goal:** reinforce and stabilize B2 performance so it becomes faster, more reliable, and less brittle under pressure.

**Main grammar/language targets:** no new heavy-grammar block; instead, mixed recycling of word order, tense choice, passives, relative clauses, connector choice, summarizing language, pronunciation/prosody, and register shifting.

**Vocabulary and thematic areas:** integrated everyday Swedish across home, health, travel, society, relationships, digital life, media, and practical work/study situations.

**Communicative functions:** repair misunderstandings, paraphrase when a word is missing, conclude a discussion, summarize decisions, soften disagreement, negotiate, and respond more spontaneously.

**Listening goals:** cope with less scaffolded audio, slightly faster delivery, and variation in rhythm or interaction pattern while still extracting gist and useful detail.

**Writing/production goals:** produce timed short texts, revise phrasing, reformulate from English into more idiomatic Swedish, and combine summary plus opinion in one response.

**Common learner difficulties:** fossilized B1 patterns returning under pressure, lingering article/gender problems, fatigue in longer listening, and difficulty sounding natural while staying accurate.

**Four-week breakdown:** mixed scenario recycling; mixed source and opinion recycling; pronunciation/register confidence; final integrated review. This stage is intentionally a consolidation stage, not a new grammar stage.

## Stage-by-stage weekly breakdown

### Stage 1: Early B1+ Expansion

**Week 1 — sentence architecture and everyday updates.** Focus on reliable Swedish sentence shape: main-clause inversion after time/place phrases, question formation, negation placement, and the most basic linker set. Keep the week inside highly practical situations such as messages, lateness, transport, and daily scheduling. The seven daily lessons should rotate through: a short scheduling dialogue, a second dialogue about a change of plan, listening for times and reasons, grammar noticing on word order, translation with time adverbials, a short spoken recap, and a weekly review.

**Week 2 — past events, present relevance, and future plans.** Focus on *preteritum* versus *perfekt*, then add common future patterns and time markers. Keep the situations adult and concrete: what happened yesterday, what has happened so far this week, what will happen this weekend, and whether plans are fixed or tentative. The week should culminate in a short narrative plus a plan-making exchange.

**Week 3 — requests, obligations, and practical problem solving.** Focus on modal verbs, advice, obligation, permission, and practical service dialogue. Use adult topics such as appointments, health basics, returns, deliveries, and home problems. The week should include both direct request language and softer polite forms, so that the learner can hear and produce *kan du…*, *skulle du kunna…*, *jag behöver…*, *du borde…*.

**Week 4 — comparison, explanation, and short opinions.** Focus on comparatives/superlatives, short reason chains, and first structured opinions. Good themes are flats, transport choices, food habits, free-time options, and simple course or service decisions. The weekly product should be a short spoken comparison and a short written opinion with at least one contrast and one reason.

### Stage 2: Solid B1+ to Low B2 Development

**Week 1 — subordinate clauses and longer explanations.** Focus on expanding with *att, eftersom, om, när, innan, medan,* and *fastän*, while keeping explanations practical and adult. Situations can include family logistics, work/study planning, schedule conflicts, and explaining changed intentions. The week should move from sentence-level control to longer spoken turns in which the learner gives conditions, reasons, and exceptions without collapsing back into short B1 fragments.

**Week 2 — reporting information and describing processes.** Focus on reporting verbs such as *säga, berätta, förklara, undra,* and *påstå*, together with short summaries of what someone else said, and the first practical use of passive forms. Good scenarios are public notices, instructions, procedures, announcements, and retelling a conversation. The learner should end the week able to explain a process and retell information from another speaker.

**Week 3 — consumer, civic, and digital life.** Focus on service problems, subscriptions, online accounts, forms, public information, and polite complaint language. Grammar emphasis should fall on passives in notices and instructions, reference language, and more varied logical connectors such as *dessutom, därför, däremot,* and *samtidigt*. This is where the learner starts sounding more competent in real adult Swedish beyond face-to-face small talk.

**Week 4 — low-B2 opinion building.** Focus on short but organized viewpoint expression: claim, reason, example, contrast, and conclusion. Use accessible themes rather than abstract academic ones: media habits, city versus smaller town, remote versus in-person routines, healthy habits, prices, and everyday digital dependence. The weekly product should be a short pros-and-cons text plus a recorded spoken viewpoint.

### Stage 3: B2-Level Complexity and Independence

**Week 1 — source-based understanding and summary.** Focus on understanding a short article, public-information text, or audio clip, then summarizing it in the learner’s own words. Teach source-framing expressions like *enligt texten*, *det viktigaste är att*, and *hon menar att*. This week is crucial because B2 increasingly depends on being able to process input and then speak or write from it, not only from personal experience.

**Week 2 — argumentation and counterargument.** Focus on controlled debate language: agreement, partial agreement, contrast, concession, and basic hypothetical reasoning. Keep topics adult but broad, such as public transport, daily environmental choices, study/work balance, digital rules, and local decisions. The learner should practice responding to another person’s opinion instead of simply stating a prepared opinion monologue.

**Week 3 — reviews, reactions, and implied meaning.** Focus on discussing films, books, events, restaurants, or travel experiences in a more B2-like way: not just “I liked it,” but why, for whom, compared with what, and with what limitations. Include listening tasks where the learner has to infer attitude, disappointment, enthusiasm, or hesitation from language and tone. This week also strengthens reaction language and more natural evaluation phrases.

**Week 4 — integrated B2 performance in adult scenarios.** Focus on multi-step, less predictable situations: moving, travel disruption, healthcare follow-up, a complicated support issue, or study/work coordination. The learner should now combine summary, explanation, stance, and solution-finding in the same lesson sequence. This is the best point for dialogues that require decision-making rather than just transactional exchange.

### Stage 4: Consolidation, Fluency, Confidence, and Integrated Review

**Week 1 — consolidating high-frequency B2 patterns.** Focus on targeted recycling of the most load-bearing structures: word order, tense choice, clause linking, passives, reporting, and viewpoint phrases. Themes should be mixed on purpose so the learner has to retrieve Swedish across contexts rather than inside one narrow semantic field. By this point, lesson design should emphasize quick recognition and quick reformulation.

**Week 2 — fluency, repair, and interaction under pressure.** Focus on paraphrasing, asking for clarification, buying time, checking understanding, and steering conversation back on track. Typical phrases include *Hur menar du?*, *Jag menar att…*, *Det är svårt att förklara, men…*, and *Om jag förstår dig rätt…*. This week is about interactional survival at a higher level, which is one of the biggest differences between classroom B1 and usable B2.

**Week 3 — confidence with register, nuance, and pronunciation.** Focus on saying the same thing more casually or more formally, strengthening pronunciation in meaningful chunks, and improving sentence stress for clarity. Pair spoken dialogues with brief written responses so the learner notices differences between conversational Swedish and more organized semi-formal written Swedish. This week should improve confidence rather than introduce major new forms.

**Week 4 — integrated review and capstone.** Focus on cumulative performance. No heavy new grammar should appear here. Instead, each daily lesson should combine understanding, summarizing, reacting, translating, and reformulating across the major themes already covered. The learner should finish the stage able to hear a short real-world dialogue or monologue, explain the main point, state a view, and respond in workable B2 Swedish.

## Guidance for converting the progression into daily dialogue-based lessons

The cleanest way to split this document into 112 daily lessons is to treat each week as one micro-syllabus with one dominant theme, one dominant grammar area, one listening objective, and one end-of-week production outcome. The daily lessons should then spiral within that week rather than jump randomly. That structure fits the CEFR action-oriented logic, where learners use language to complete meaningful tasks, and it fits the Swedish syllabi emphasis on rich opportunities to listen, speak, read, write, and reflect.

A practical seven-day lesson arc is:

**Day 1:** introduce the week’s situation with one short dialogue and core vocabulary.  
**Day 2:** add a second dialogue in the same theme but with a different communicative angle.  
**Day 3:** do grammar noticing from the dialogue transcript; keep the explanation short and contrastive.  
**Day 4:** run English-to-Swedish translation and guided reformulation on the same material.  
**Day 5:** use a slightly less scaffolded listening built from the same weekly theme.  
**Day 6:** require short production: summarize, retell, compare, recommend, or respond.  
**Day 7:** recycle the week together with one element from an earlier week, so Stage 4 will later feel like a natural culmination rather than an abrupt review week.  

This arc keeps grammar attached to meaning, preserves dialogue and listening as the engine of the lesson, and creates the repeated retrieval needed for the move from B1 knowledge to B2 usable control.

For JSON conversion, each daily lesson should store at least: stage, week, day, communicative goal, dialogue setting, active vocabulary, recycled vocabulary, grammar focus, listening focus, production target, and review links. A good default is one main grammar point plus one recycled grammar point, and one main dialogue function rather than several unrelated ones. This keeps payloads compact and app logic predictable.

Dialogue design should stay close to standard spoken Swedish, especially in Stages 1 to 3. Natural contractions, hesitations, and discourse particles can gradually increase, but dialect-heavy material should be reserved for optional enrichment. Across the whole progression, the learner should repeatedly hear and produce sentences like *Jag vet att hon inte kommer*, *Jag har redan bokat tiden*, *Det beror på vad du behöver*, and *Å ena sidan är det billigare, men å andra sidan tar det längre tid*. These are the kinds of reusable frames that carry a learner from B1 to B2.

Finally, the quality test for every daily lesson is simple: after the lesson, can the learner understand a short Swedish exchange, explain what is happening, and say or write something slightly more organized than before? If yes, the lesson belongs in this progression. If it only teaches isolated grammar or isolated vocabulary, it does not.
